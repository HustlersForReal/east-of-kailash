Stamp Duty, Registration and Mutation in South Delhi: The Real Cost of Buying

July 28, 202610 min read

Male 6%, female 4%, joint 5% — plus 1% registration. Then the MCD e-mutation. The full post-deal money trail, with the documents the sub-registrar and the MCD actually ask for.

Most buyers budget the property price and forget the government’s share until the week of registration. In Delhi that share is large, gender-linked and non-negotiable — and after registration, a second process (mutation) decides whose name the municipal records show. This guide maps the complete money trail for a South Delhi purchase: what you pay, to whom, in what order, and which papers to keep ready.

Stamp duty: the gender-linked slabs

Delhi’s stamp duty on a sale deed is 6% for a male buyer, 4% for a female buyer and 5% for joint ownership — calculated on the actual sale price or the circle-rate value of the property, whichever is higher. Registration charges are a flat 1% of the deal value for everyone, plus a nominal pasting charge. So a man registering a property effectively pays 7% over the price, a woman 5%, and a jointly-owned purchase 6%. On an East of Kailash builder floor running into crores, registering in a woman’s name is a fully legal saving of several lakhs — families here use this concession routinely.

  • Male buyer: 6% stamp duty + 1% registration = 7% total outflow
  • Female buyer: 4% stamp duty + 1% registration = 5% total outflow
  • Joint (male + female): 5% stamp duty + 1% registration = 6% total outflow
  • Duty applies to the higher of deal price and circle-rate value — under-declaring the price never reduces duty

Registration: what actually happens at the sub-registrar

The sale deed is executed and registered at the sub-registrar’s office of the concerned zone, with stamp duty paid upfront (today largely through online/stock-holding channels) and both parties present with ID proof, photographs and witnesses. Keep the registered deed’s number, date and sub-registrar details safe — every downstream step, from mutation to home-loan disbursement to future resale, references them. One practical tip: verify the circle-rate value of your exact property before the appointment, so there is no last-minute duty shortfall at the counter.

Mutation: the step buyers skip (and regret)

Registration transfers title; mutation (e-Change of Name with the MCD) updates the municipal property-tax record to show you as the taxpayer. Under Section 128 of the DMC Act, 1957, mutation is granted for property-tax realisation only — it does not by itself confer legal title — but without it, tax bills, and eventually utility linkages, stay in the seller’s name. Since 2019, deeds registered at the sub-registrar are auto-pushed to the MCD and mutation is substantially auto-triggered, though buyers should still log in, verify the UPIC ledger and download the e-Change of Name certificate rather than assume it happened.

The mutation file: documents the MCD asks for

  • Complete registered sale/conveyance deed (not just first and last pages)
  • Chain of previous ownership documents, where not already reflected in MCD records
  • Latest property-tax receipt showing zero arrears — mutation is normally held up until dues are cleared
  • Buyer ID proof (Aadhaar/PAN/passport) plus applicant and property photographs
  • Notarised affidavit (Rs 10 stamp paper) and indemnity bond (Rs 100 stamp paper) in MCD formats
  • For inheritance cases: death certificate, will or succession paperwork, and surviving-member/NOC documentation as applicable

“Registration makes you the owner. Mutation makes the city agree with you. Budget an hour for the second the week you finish the first.”

Timelines and trouble spots

A clean sale-deed mutation is typically processed within 15–30 working days; deficiency flags (illegible deed scans, wrong UPIC, missing affidavit) come back by SMS/email for correction, while rival claims or litigation can stretch cases to months with a hearing before the zonal assessing officer. Older deeds (pre-2019), gift and relinquishment transfers, and inheritance files always need a manual application on the MCD portal rather than relying on auto-trigger.
